# Search Pattern Probe 009 — SOURCE-CLAIM LADDER

STATUS: CANDIDATE_REUSABLE_FUNCTION
TEST_OBJECT: The Legend of Sleepy Hollow
PURPOSE: Expand the Search Universe; Sleepy Hollow is pressure-test material, not the senior product.

## FUNCTION
When a search encounters a claim about origin, inspiration, identity, or influence, do not treat all sources as equal. Climb a source-claim ladder:

1. Locate the primary work/text.
2. Locate institutional/bibliographic evidence that fixes work, edition, date, authorship, and provenance.
3. Locate specialist scholarship that explicitly argues the claimed relationship.
4. Locate competing or additional source roads.
5. Label each conclusion by evidence strength: ESTABLISHED / SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION / PLAUSIBLE / SPECULATIVE / UNRESOLVED.
6. Preserve multiple roads when the evidence does not justify collapsing them into one origin story.

## WHY THIS ADDS A DISTINCT SEARCH FUNCTION
Earlier branching searches deliberately open several candidate roads. SOURCE-CLAIM LADDER adds a different function: it grades the evidentiary status of each road so a plausible parallel is not silently promoted into established provenance.

## PRESSURE TEST
Library of Congress records anchor Irving's work and editions. PMLA scholarship explicitly discusses both American folk traditions and German-source scholarship, while other historical/literary accounts describe several possible European and local influences. The useful reusable function is therefore not 'pick the best origin'; it is 'separate evidence classes and preserve unresolved plurality.'

## SEARCH DOORS EXERCISED
- Primary/institutional text
- Library/bibliographic record
- Academic/specialist scholarship
- Literary/historical organization
- Contradiction / alternative-road check

## FAILURE MODE PREVENTED
First-plausible-source lock-in: finding one attractive influence claim and rewriting it as proven fact.

## REUSABILITY FIT
Suitable for books, genealogy, historical claims, technical provenance, policy origins, scientific attribution, product-history research, and any search where claims have unequal evidence strength.
