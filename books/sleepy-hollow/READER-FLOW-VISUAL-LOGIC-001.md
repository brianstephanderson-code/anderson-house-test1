## Core production question

For every section:

**Would a visual interruption improve the reader's experience, or damage the text's momentum / ambiguity / comic timing?**

This layer does not choose final illustrations. It classifies opportunity.

---

## Classification

- **OPEN** — illustration may strengthen the section.
- **STRONG** — illustration likely performs a useful reader function.
- **CAUTION** — image may work, but timing is delicate.
- **AVOID** — uninterrupted text is probably stronger here.
- **TRANSITION** — visual/page-turn may help at the boundary rather than inside the section.

## READER-FLOW ZONES

### ZONE A — Opening atmosphere
S01-S04

Goal:
- immerse slowly
- do not over-explain
- allow one or two strong visual anchors
- preserve haze

### ZONE B — Character comedy
S05-S08

Goal:
- reinforce Ichabod's physical/comic identity
- avoid illustrating every joke
- let verbal satire carry most of the rhythm

### ZONE C — Desire and rivalry
S09-S12

Goal:
- show motive and contrast
- avoid turning the tale into conventional romance art

### ZONE D — Party convergence
S13-S18

Goal:
- use visuals as structural breathing points
- feast and ghost-story hinge are stronger than blanket coverage
- preserve the speed and social energy of dance/reversal

### ZONE E — Night terror
S19-S25

Goal:
- fewer illustrations, more strategic placement
- preserve acceleration
- use page-turn logic rather than frequent full-page pauses
- do not resolve the rider too early

### ZONE F — Aftermath and ambiguity
S26-S30

Goal:
- let evidence reframe the chase
- restore comic distance
- end with atmosphere rather than a definitive answer

## VISUAL DENSITY PRINCIPLE

The edition should **not** aim for one illustration every fixed number of pages.

Instead:

**VISUAL DENSITY FOLLOWS FUNCTION.**

Likely pattern:
- opening: moderate
- character build: moderate
- social middle: moderate
- chase: sparse
- aftermath: moderate
- closing: one atmospheric image possible

This is intentionally uneven.
